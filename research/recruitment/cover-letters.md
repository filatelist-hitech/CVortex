# Cover Letters

## Finding

The evidence does not support either universal statement:

- “cover letter is always required”; or
- “nobody reads cover letters”.

Usage depends on employer, application flow, market and role.

## Evidence

### UK

The UK National Careers Service recommends including a cover letter with a CV and gives a concise multi-paragraph structure. This is official candidate guidance, not proof that every UK employer requires or reads one.

Source: https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/covering-letter

### Indeed

Indeed’s candidate guidance distinguishes employers that require a cover letter, make it optional or provide no upload path. This directly supports context-dependent behavior.

Source: https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/when-not-to-include-a-cover-letter

### Russia / HeadHunter

HeadHunter guidance treats a cover letter as complementary to the resume and useful for explaining motivation/value in the response process.

Source: https://feedback.hh.ru/knowledge-base/article/1846

## Technical roles

No high-quality evidence found that technical candidates universally benefit from long narrative letters. For technical hiring, concise personalization around fit, motivation, domain relevance and a small number of truthful proof points is safer than generic prose.

Confidence: MEDIUM.

## Recommendation candidate for CVortex

Generate cover letters conditionally:

- **required**: generate and prominently surface;
- **optional but high-value**: generate short + standard variants;
- **explicitly not requested / no field**: do not treat missing letter as a defect;
- **unknown**: offer a short option without claiming necessity.

All candidate claims still follow `Generated Content → Claims → confirmed Career Facts`.

## Useful length

Research did not establish one universal word count. Prefer concise role-specific content and adapt to the target platform/employer instructions.

## Personalization dimensions

When evidence exists:

- why this role;
- why this company/domain;
- 2–3 strongest confirmed matches;
- one relevant achievement or proof point;
- explicit hard-gap handling only when strategically useful and truthful.

Avoid generic “I am passionate and results-driven” filler that adds no evidence.
